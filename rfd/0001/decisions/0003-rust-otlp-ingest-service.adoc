= Decision 0003: Rust OTLP ingest service
:status: accepted
:date: 2026-11-09

link:../README.adoc[RFD 0001] · link:0001-poc-physical-layout.adoc[Decision 0001] ·
link:0002-single-tenant-core.adoc[Decision 0002]

This record replaces the server-side OpenTelemetry Collector distribution
(`toudai-collector`) with `toudai-ingest`, a Rust OTLP ingest service in this
repository. It changes what an OTLP success response means: success is
returned only after both destinations of an accepted record have committed.
It amends Decision 0001 D5 and the Decision 0002 consequences, and it reverses
the 2026-10-17 owner call, not recorded in the RFD, to keep the Collector build
until the qualification stage.

== Context

On 2026-10-17 the Collector build was kept, and the choice between it and a
Rust ingest service was deferred to qualification, once acknowledgement and
backpressure numbers existed. On 2026-11-09 that call was reversed. The
reasons below do not rest on tenancy gaps or on the placeholder key files;
both are temporary and would not justify either choice.

* Toudai already owns three of the four server-side components: the exporter,
  the auth extension, and the gateway processor. Only the OTLP receiver is
  stock.
* Acknowledgement, retry, and backpressure are today a combination of stock
  Collector settings. Experiment 018 found that, with a sending queue enabled
  as the gateway configuration has it, an OTLP success response means
  admission to that queue, and Experiment 021 measured the
  consequences: requests admitted to a stalled in-memory queue were lost when
  the process stopped, a permanent canonical failure was dropped after the
  retry budget while the producer saw 200, and a lost serving response
  duplicated both destinations because the retry re-ran both inserts. Owning
  the service makes each of these code Toudai controls and tests.
* The DDL, the writer, and the reader live in one repository. The collector
  repository carries a vendored copy of the DDL that has to be refreshed by
  hand; a writer in this repository applies `clickhouse/schema` directly.
* The otelcol-contrib pin treadmill goes away. The direction review named it as
  a maintenance risk.
* One language and one CI.

Honeycomb's hosted ingest is the reference shape: standard SDKs and stock
Collectors send OTLP to an owned endpoint, and the endpoint returns success
only after the event is durable (for Honeycomb, written to its replicated
Kafka log). Toudai's PoC has no log in front of ClickHouse, so its only durable
point before overflow exists is the ClickHouse commit.

The costs are stated rather than argued away:

* OTLP receiver conformance becomes Toudai's to test. The stock receivers gave
  it for free.
* Server-side Collector processors are no longer available in the gateway. A
  customer who wants them can still run a Collector in front of Toudai.
* The single-tenant collector work (collector repository #5) is temporary.

== Decisions

=== D1. Component

`toudai-ingest`, a Rust crate at `ingest/` in this repository, is the
server-side OTLP endpoint. It receives OTLP logs and traces over gRPC and
HTTP, applies the key and dataset rules of Decision 0002 D3, assigns
`event_id`, and writes the Decision 0001 layout, as amended by Decision 0002
D2, with its split canonical payload table. OTLP metrics stay out of scope, as
they are in the Collector build. "The gateway" in Decision 0001 D5 and
Decision 0002 D3 now means this service. Customers keep the same wire
contract: they send OTLP from stock SDKs or Collectors with the same
`X-Toudai-Key` and `X-Toudai-Dataset` headers. What a success response means
changes as in D2.

=== D2. Acknowledgement

Amends Decision 0001 D5, which did not fix the acknowledgement point; the
Collector's sending queue set it (Experiments 018 and 021).

* A request succeeds only after the canonical and serving inserts holding all
  of its accepted records have committed. Concurrent requests may share one
  insert, either by group commit in the service or by a ClickHouse async
  insert with `wait_for_async_insert`; each request is answered when the
  insert holding its rows commits. Either mechanism satisfies this decision,
  and the one-hour 10k events/s run chooses between them.
* Records rejected individually, such as one with a duplicate attribute key,
  are reported in OTLP `partial_success` with a fixed message instead of
  being dropped silently.
* Backpressure and unavailability are retryable responses, so the producer's
  own retry queue is the buffer: HTTP 429 with `Retry-After` or gRPC
  `UNAVAILABLE` when the service is full, and HTTP 503 or gRPC `UNAVAILABLE`
  when ClickHouse cannot take the write.
* In-service retry is bounded by the request deadline and retries only the
  destination that failed. A producer retry after a failed request assigns new
  `event_id`s, as Decision 0001 D5 already allows.
* Durable overflow to object storage, which would let the service acknowledge
  while ClickHouse is down, remains a qualification item together with
  catch-up.

=== D3. Bounds

* Request body limits stay at the Collector's defaults: 20 MiB for OTLP/HTTP
  and 4 MiB for OTLP/gRPC. A compressed body on either protocol is also
  capped at the same decompressed size, so a compression bomb cannot exceed
  it.
* An admission budget in bytes bounds memory: a request holds its share from
  decode until the insert holding its rows commits, and a full budget is
  answered with the backpressure response of D2.

=== D4. Unchanged

* The Decision 0001 layout as amended by Decision 0002 D2, the
  canonical-first pair of ordered inserts,
  gateway-assigned UUIDv7 `event_id`, and at-least-once delivery with no
  deduplication. The two inserts are still not atomic; a lost response on an
  insert that did commit still duplicates that destination.
* The Decision 0002 D3 rules in full: key validity with an optional dataset
  list; dataset resolution from the header, else the key's single dataset,
  else a configured default; refusal of a dataset outside the key's list
  whatever its source; acceptance of a producer `toudai.dataset` resource
  attribute only when it matches the resolved dataset; and rejection of a
  producer-supplied `toudai.event_id`.
* Static key files remain the placeholder for the key store. Write keys use
  the same `<key> [<dataset>...]` file format as read keys, in a separate file;
  which file a key is in decides its permission.

=== D5. Collector repository

Collector #5 merges as the Decision 0002 write path and stays the PoC write
path until `toudai-ingest` reaches parity. After it merges, the collector
repository is frozen: it takes security fixes, pin fixes, and dataset-rule
changes kept in step with `toudai-ingest`, and no new features. Parity is
shown by Experiment 025, which sends one fixture through both paths and
replays the Experiment 021 failure cases against `toudai-ingest`. The
repository is then marked deprecated and archived, not deleted, because
Experiment 024 and the acceptance and tenant-policy `-impl` notes link its
commits.

== Consequences

These amend the records and plans below; they are not rewritten here.

* Decision 0002 Consequences, the bullet on the owned exporter, auth
  extension, gateway processor, and vendored DDL: these become modules of
  `toudai-ingest`, and the vendored DDL goes away.
* link:../acceptance-and-replay.adoc[Acceptance and replay], "Collector-first
  PoC" and "Reuse and Toudai ownership": Collector reuse moves to the customer
  side. The server-side endpoint is owned.
* link:../product-contract.adoc[Product contract], "Implementation language":
  the sentence "Reusing the OTel Collector does not require rewriting its
  components in Rust" no longer describes the plan.
* The README PoC completion gate diagram shows `toudai-ingest` in place of
  Collector authentication and validation plus the owned exporter.
* The acknowledgement and duplicate items in the `-impl` checklists are tested
  against `toudai-ingest`.
* The one-hour 10k events/s run targets `toudai-ingest`, since that is the
  path that will ship, and chooses between the two insert mechanisms of D2.

== Superseding

A later decision record may revise any item above. Returning success before
the ClickHouse commit needs a durable write point in front of it, such as the
qualification overflow path, named with its loss and duplicate measurements.
